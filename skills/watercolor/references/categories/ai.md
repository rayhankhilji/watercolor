# Playbook: AI products

The category is drowning in sameness: dark backgrounds, purple-blue gradients, sparkles, glowing orbs, "your AI copilot". Distinctiveness here is unusually cheap and unusually valuable.

## First, classify the AI company
- **AI-native tool for a job** (writing, meetings, dictation, coding, design): the job and the person matter more than the AI. Brand like a great tool in that job's world; AI is the engine, not the hero.
- **AI infrastructure / models / platforms**: brand like serious infrastructure — precision, research credibility, developer respect.
- **AI companion / consumer**: brand like a consumer product — emotional, human, warm; careful with anthropomorphism.

## What great looks like
- **Show the outcome, not the magic.** Real before/after, real transcripts, real output. Demos (videos/GIFs/Lottie captured in `media`) are often the best brand asset — reference them.
- **Human warmth** in a cold category: warm neutrals, serif accents, real photography of people working, handwritten or paper textures.
- **Calm intelligence**: restrained motion, typographic confidence, few colors — trust comes from composure.
- **A metaphor that isn't a brain or a robot**: voice → waveforms, memory → notebooks/paper, speed → typographic motion.

## Traps (all slop here)
✨ sparkles, robot/brain icons, purple-indigo gradients, "unlock the power of AI", "your AI-powered X", neon on black, glowing orbs, chat-bubble everything, fake "AI is typing…" animations, abstract 3D renders.

## Voice
Plain-spoken and concrete. Say what it does in the user's words ("Write 4× faster in any app"), name limits honestly, avoid hype adjectives. Speed and accuracy claims need numbers. Second person.

## Motion
Purposeful and quick — showing the product working (text appearing, audio becoming text) is the motion signature; ambient loops should be subtle.
