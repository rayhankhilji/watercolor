# Playbook: B2B SaaS

**Buyer ≠ user.** Most B2B brands talk to two people: the economic buyer (VP, CFO, Head of X — wants risk reduction, ROI, proof) and the daily user (wants speed, clarity, craft). Identify both from the copy: "Contact sales"/"Book a demo"/SOC 2/customer logos speak to the buyer; product screenshots, keyboard shortcuts, templates speak to the user. Write both into `company.audience`.

## What great looks like
- **Proof density**: customer logos, named case studies, real numbers ("99.999% uptime", "$1.4T processed"). The extractor gathers `company.customers` and `company.stats` — use them in copy and slides.
- **Product as hero**: real UI, cropped and staged with care, not fake dashboards.
- **One action color**: a single CTA color everywhere; secondary actions are outlined or text.
- **Calm neutrals** with a temperature (cool blue-gray for trust/finance, warm gray for creative tools).
- **Precise type**: a grotesk or neo-grotesk with tuned tracking; a mono for technical accents.

## Common traps
- Bento grids of feature cards with generic icons.
- "All-in-one platform" positioning; feature lists as pillars.
- Enterprise beige: everything safe, nothing remembered.
- Over-animated hero sections that slow down a buyer who wants facts.

## Voice
Confident, specific, outcome-led. Lead with the job done, then the mechanism, then proof. Sentence case. Numbers as numerals. Calm enthusiasm — no exclamation marks.

## Distinctive moves that work
- A signature surface tied to the product metaphor (Stripe's flowing gradient = money in motion; Linear's dark precision = engineering focus).
- Editorial typography in a technical category (a serif accent).
- Opinionated illustration system instead of stock (Notion, Mailchimp).
- A memorable color in a sea of blue (Gumroad pink, Figma's multicolor, Plaid's black).
